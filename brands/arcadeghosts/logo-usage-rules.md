# ArcadeGhosts Logo Usage Rules

This is the practical guardrail doc for using the ArcadeGhosts logo across print, merch, and social outputs.

## What The Logo Needs To Do

The logo should feel:

- recognizable first
- atmospheric second
- playful without becoming sloppy
- bold enough to anchor a layout without swallowing it

If an output makes the logo feel muddy, cramped, stretched, or decorative-only, it is probably drifting.

## Preferred Usage

- use the PNG asset when print compatibility or broad tool support matters
- place the mark on dark layered backgrounds where its silhouette stays legible
- give it enough breathing room that it reads as a mark, not texture
- let the logo be either a focal element or a quiet anchor, not both at once

## Clear Space

- keep at least `22%` of the logo width as clear space around the mark
- never use less than `28px` of visual breathing room in digital outputs
- if the logo sits inside a panel, that panel should still respect the same padding

## Sizing

- absolute minimum digital width: `110px`
- comfortable standard digital width: `180px`
- hero or OG-style focal usage: `320px` or larger

If the logo has to go smaller than the minimum, it should probably be replaced by text or removed.

## Good Backgrounds

- dark night gradients
- muted neon panels
- foggy, pine-heavy, or diner-adjacent imagery with strong contrast
- restrained atmospheric surfaces that support the mark

## Bad Backgrounds

- flat white
- loud rainbow or candy gradients
- dense visual noise
- bright photos with weak edge contrast

## Good Treatments

- contained brand panel
- upper-corner anchor
- hero focal block with enough margin
- subtle shadow or contrast support when needed for legibility

## Avoid

- stretching or squashing the logo
- repeating the logo as wallpaper
- piling on glow until the edges blur
- tracing it with novelty outlines
- placing long copy directly over or too close to the mark
- using it as a tiny unreadable sticker in a crowded composition

## Generator Rule Of Thumb

Future generators should default to:

- dark backgrounds
- one logo instance per surface unless a layout truly needs more
- readable copy hierarchy before decorative effects
- logo placements that feel intentional, not like last-minute badges
