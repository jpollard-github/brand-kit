# Print Proofing

Reference: 2026-07-03 EDT

Use this before ordering any printed collateral from `brand-kit`.

This standard exists because a card can look branded and premium while still failing the real-world readability test.

Business cards are not websites.
Business cards are not brochures.
Business cards are invitations.

They should be readable at `18` to `30` inches, in imperfect light, by someone who is not already invested.

The card's job is to:

- make Jason memorable
- establish trust
- communicate one clear idea
- make someone visit the website

The card's job is not to:

- explain every service
- summarize the website
- answer every question
- sell the entire business

`brand-kit` owns physical proofing and card design rules.
`consulting-business` should eventually reference this standard when generating client-facing collateral work orders, but the design rules stay here.

Recommended downstream note:

`Client-facing print or networking collateral should use brand-kit print proofing standards before ordering. Brand-kit owns physical proofing and card design rules.`

## Three Second Test

Every printed piece must answer this question:

`What should someone remember after looking at this for three seconds?`

If reviewers cannot answer this quickly, the design is probably trying to communicate too much.

Work With Me card:

- `Jason Pollard`
- `Small Software & Workflow Improvements`
- QR code

Personal card:

- `ArcadeGhosts`
- interesting logo
- QR code

## One Impossible To Miss Thing

Every printed artifact should contain one dominant visual anchor.

Examples for a business card:

- Jason's name
- `ArcadeGhosts`
- QR code

Not all three competing equally.

This is a visual-hierarchy rule, not a font-size trick.
If the design has no obvious anchor, it is probably trying to do too much.

## Physical Proofing Checklist

- print at `100%` actual size before ordering
- hold it naturally in one hand
- read it while standing
- read it from desk distance
- view from `18 inches`
- view from `24 inches`
- view from `36 inches`
- read it under normal indoor lighting
- read it outdoors
- view it in dim light
- view it on a phone camera photo
- verify all URLs
- scan the QR code from the printed proof
- ignore the QR code and confirm the URL still works as a fallback
- check that name, URL, and offer are readable without squinting
- hand it to another person and ask what they notice first
- ask another person what they think this person does
- ask another person whether they would keep the card
- ask what could be removed
- confirm the card has one job, not five
- confirm text is not doing the website's job
- confirm no placeholder text remains
- confirm the card feels like ArcadeGhosts

## Minimum Readability Rules

- primary name and title should be readable at `24` to `36` inches
- URLs should be readable at `18` to `24` inches
- avoid body copy below practical print size
- prefer fewer words over smaller text
- QR code must be large enough to scan easily
- if the card needs explanation, the card is too busy

Practical readability matters more than pretending exact font-size rules alone solve the problem.
Removing content is usually better than shrinking fonts.

## Decision

Decision recorded: `2026-07-03 EDT`

Version 1 business cards validated the ArcadeGhosts visual identity, especially the premium black-stock and neon direction, but they also proved that information density and proofing matter more than adding additional copy.

Future print artifacts should prioritize clarity, hierarchy, and curiosity over completeness.
